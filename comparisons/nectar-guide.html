"They'll Drink It" ≠ "It's What Nature Makes"

🎯 The Real Question

Will hummingbirds drink plain sugar water? Absolutely. They'll drink anything with calories. That doesn't make it what wildflowers produce.

Here's what wildflower analysis reveals: Trumpet vine, cardinal flower, and bee balm don't just produce "sugar water." They produce specific blends of three sugars. As long as these flowers have existed, they've produced this composition. Hummingbirds seek these flowers specifically.

Plain sugar water = one sugar (sucrose).
Wildflower nectar = three sugars in specific ratios.

Both will feed hummingbirds. One matches what nature designed. We replicated the blend, not just the sweetness.

Why Sugar Ratio Matters

Ratio Concentration Natural Source Best For Energy Value
3:1 25% Desert flowers, high-sugar blooms Arid climates, migration fueling Higher (more calories)
4:1 20% Eastern forest flowers (trumpet vine, cardinal flower) Most US species, daily feeding Moderate (optimal for digestion)
5:1 16.7% Dilute flowers Not recommended Lower
🔬 The Science: Hummingbirds have fast metabolisms but also sensitive kidneys. Too concentrated (3:1) can cause dehydration in humid climates. Too dilute (5:1) doesn't provide enough energy. The 4:1 ratio matches the nectar of their preferred flowers in eastern forests.
