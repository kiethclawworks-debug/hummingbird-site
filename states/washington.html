🌿 WA Native Flowers for Hummingbirds

These Pacific Northwest natives evolved alongside Rufous Hummingbirds:

  • Red-Flowering Currant (Ribes sanguineum) — Blooms March-May when Rufous arrive. The signature flower of the PNW spring.
  • Red Columbine (Aquilegia formosa) — Red/yellow bells April-August, streambanks and moist forests.
  • Salmonberry (Rubus spectabilis) — Pink/magenta blooms March-May, western WA forests.
  • Penstemons — Blue/purple/pink May-July, eastern WA primarily.
  • Indian Paintbrush — Orange/red May-July, mountain meadows and eastern WA.
💡 Pro Tip: Plant red-flowering currant near your feeders. It blooms exactly when Rufous arrive, providing natural nectar at the critical early-season moment.

🎭 Dealing With Aggression

Rufous Hummingbirds are the most territorial in North America. One male may try to guard an entire yard.

Solutions:

  • Multiple feeders — Place them out of sight from each other
  • More feeders = less fighting — When one bird can't defend them all, others get to eat
  • Cluster strategy — Some people put 6+ feeders in one area so no single bird can dominate
💡 Fun Fact: Rufous Hummingbirds have been known to chase away chipmunks, jays, and even people from "their" feeders. They're tiny but fearless!
