🌸 Texas is Big — Flowers Vary By Region

Texas has three distinct ecological regions for hummingbirds. The wildflowers (and our nectar blends) match each region:

East Texas (Piney Woods):

  • Primary flowers: Trumpet vine, cardinal flower, coral honeysuckle
  • Sugar profile: Sucrose-dominant 3-sugar blend, 20% concentration
  • Primary bird: Ruby-throated Hummingbird
  • Recommended nectar: Ruby-Throated Blend (4:1)

Central Texas (Hill Country):

  • Primary flowers: Texas betony, autumn sage, coral honeysuckle
  • Sugar profile: Mixed blend, 20% concentration
  • Primary bird: Black-chinned Hummingbird (breeding)
  • Recommended nectar: Black-Chinned Blend (4:1)

West Texas (Trans-Pecos):

  • Primary flowers: Ocotillo, desert willow, red yucca, chuparosa
  • Sugar profile: Desert-adapted blend, 22-25% concentration
  • Primary bird: Black-chinned, Anna's (El Paso area)
  • Recommended nectar: Black-Chinned Blend (4:1) or Anna's Formula (3:1) for far west
💡 The Regional Strategy: If you're in Houston or Tyler, you want the eastern blend (Ruby-Throated). If you're in Austin, San Antonio, or El Paso, you want the western/southern blend (Black-Chinned or Anna's). Texas is big enough to need different formulas!

🌿 Texas Native Flowers for Hummingbirds

Texas has incredible native diversity. Here are the best by region:

Statewide Winners:

  • Coral Honeysuckle (Lonicera sempervirens) — Coral/orange March-October. Works everywhere in Texas.
  • Turk's Cap (Malvaviscus arboreus) — Red May-November, East and Central Texas.

East & Central Texas:

  • Texas Betony (Stachys coccinea) — Red March-November, Hill Country native.
  • Autumn Sage (Salvia greggii) — Red/pink March-November, drought-tolerant.

West Texas:

  • Ocotillo (Fouquieria splendens) — Red/orange after rain, iconic desert bloom.
  • Desert Willow (Chilopsis linearis) — Pink/white April-September.
  • Red Yucca (Hesperaloe parviflora) — Coral blooms, extremely drought-tolerant.
💡 Texas Gardening Tip: In hot Texas summers, plant native salvias and autumn sage. They're drought-tolerant, bloom for months, and hummingbirds love them.
