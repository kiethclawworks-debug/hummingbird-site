🌸 The Cherokee Rose Problem

Here's a secret most Georgians don't know: Our state flower isn't native to Georgia. The Cherokee Rose (Rosa laevigata) is from China. And more importantly — hummingbirds don't visit it.

What Georgia hummingbirds actually want:

  • Coral Honeysuckle — Native vine, blooms March-October
  • Native Azaleas — Sweet fragrance, blooms March-April
  • Crossvine — Orange/yellow trumpets, March-May
  • Cardinal Flower — Red spikes, July-October
💡 Georgia Irony: Our state flower is an Asian rose that hummingbirds ignore. Meanwhile, native coral honeysuckle — which feeds hummingbirds for 8 months — has no official status. Plant the native. Feed the birds.

🌿 Georgia Native Flowers for Hummingbirds

Statewide Winners:

  • Coral Honeysuckle (Lonicera sempervirens) — THE Georgia hummingbird plant. Coral/orange blooms March-October statewide. Climbs anything. Hummingbirds fight over it.
  • Crossvine (Bignonia capreolata) — Orange/yellow trumpet flowers March-May. Explosive bloomer. Climbs trees.
  • Red Buckeye (Aesculus pavia) — Red flower spikes March-April. Small understory tree. Early season critical.

North Georgia (Piedmont & Mountains):

  • Native Azaleas (Rhododendron canescens, R. prunifolium) — Sweet fragrance, pink/white/yellow March-April. Woodland edges.
  • Rhododendron — Various species, blooms spring, mountain forests.

Central & South Georgia:

  • Cardinal Flower (Lobelia cardinalis) — Red spikes July-October, moist areas statewide.
  • Trumpet Vine (Campsis radicans) — Orange/red June-September. Aggressive but effective.
  • Bee Balm (Monarda didyma) — Pink/red June-August, garden favorite.

🎭 The Crossvine vs. Trumpet Vine Choice

Both vines attract hummingbirds. Here's the difference:

  • Crossvine — Blooms early (March-May), less aggressive, better behaved in gardens
  • Trumpet Vine — Blooms mid-summer (June-September), extremely aggressive, will take over if not managed

Our recommendation: Plant crossvine near your house. Plant trumpet vine on a fence or structure away from buildings where it can run wild. Both feed hummingbirds; one is better mannered.
