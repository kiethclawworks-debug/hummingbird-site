🌸 The Camellia Problem

Alabama's state flower, the camellia, has three problems:

  1. It's from Asia — not native to Alabama
  2. It blooms in winter (November-April) — when hummingbirds are in Mexico
  3. Hummingbirds don't visit it — the flowers are designed for bees, not birds

What Alabama hummingbirds actually want:

  • Coral Honeysuckle — Blooms March-October, when hummers are here
  • Native Azaleas — Sweet fragrance, March-April, North AL forests
  • Wild Bergamot — Lavender blooms, June-August, meadows statewide
  • Cardinal Flower — Red spikes, July-October, streambanks

🌿 Alabama Native Flowers for Hummingbirds

Statewide Champions:

  • Coral Honeysuckle (Lonicera sempervirens) — Coral/orange March-October. The Alabama standard. Climbs anything, feeds hummers for 8 months.
  • Crossvine (Bignonia capreolata) — Orange/yellow March-May. Explosive early bloomer.
  • Red Buckeye (Aesculus pavia) — Red spikes March-April. Small tree, understory.

North Alabama (Bankhead Forest, Mountains):

  • Native Azaleas — Pink/white/yellow/orange March-April. Bankhead has incredible diversity.
  • Rhododendron — Various species, mountain forests.
  • Wild Bergamot — Lavender/pink June-August, meadows.

Central & South Alabama:

  • Cardinal Flower (Lobelia cardinalis) — Red July-October, moist areas.
  • Trumpet Vine — Orange/red June-September.
  • Native Honeysuckles — Various species, statewide.

Mobile Delta Region:

  • Native Honeysuckles — Climb Delta cypress and tupelo.
  • Cardinal Flower — Wetland edges.
  • Crossvine — Climbs hardwoods.
💡 Bankhead National Forest Tip: If you live in North Alabama near Bankhead, you're lucky. This forest has some of the best remaining native azalea diversity in the Southeast. The wildflowers there produce the same 3-sugar blend we replicate.
